Legs stowed sideways

Jason's ratchet legs and Jason and Chandler's passive hook, as built. Stowed legs stick out sideways, so on a slope the uphill foot catches the ground before the legs drop. The capsule then rotates on the hook to match the slope.

Change 1

Fold the legs up when stowed

Same parts, but the legs stow folded up against the capsule. Nothing touches until the legs drop, so each leg finds the ground and locks. This alone fixes 20°. At 30° the capsule still pivots on the hook.

Change 2

Add the seat ring

A collar on the crown post sits 1 mm under a lip ring on the carrier. When the first foot touches, the collar hits the lip, so the package and carrier act as one piece and the fans can hold it level.

Loose hook: hook tension T = W − F (it unloads)
Seat ring: T = W + 1.8 F (it tightens)

The hook also cannot open while the package rests on one foot. It opens once the legs share the weight.

What this does not show

No wind, ideal fan control, and hard ground only. The soft-ground part of the model does not yet match our pillow runs, so it is left out. Legs and the seat parts are simplified shapes on the real capsule, post, loop, hook, swivel and carrier plate from the team CAD. The hook opening and carrier lift after release are drawn, not simulated.
